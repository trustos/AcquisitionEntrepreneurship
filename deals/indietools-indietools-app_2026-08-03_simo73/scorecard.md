## Fit-to-build read (the team's lens)

The technology is ideal: Next.js, Tailwind, Postgres, Supabase, Vercel, Stripe is precisely this team's stack. Operating and modernizing the codebase — adding AI-powered discovery, better categorization, an API layer — is straightforward.

But operating the tech is not the problem. The problem is customer acquisition. A tool directory grows through two things: (1) SEO dominance (appearing when indie makers search for directories or tool categories) and (2) personal brand / community presence (a founder with a large, trusted audience driving submissions and listings). Both are exactly what this team does not do. The team's strength is in building product, not in climbing Google rankings or growing a Twitter/X audience from 1,270 followers to something meaningful.

The growth-lever kill gate fires. There is no engineering solution to the distribution problem here.

## Does the value transfer? (key-person check)

The founder (Alex Turovski) has 1,270 X/Twitter followers — a small personal following with limited promotional reach. The SEO component is product-based (directory pages, tool listings), not founder-post-dependent, so it conveys. The domain (indietools.app, DR 26), code, and Stripe integration all transfer cleanly.

The risk of value walking away with the founder is low, primarily because there is not much value to walk away. The ~600 free users' transferability (email list, account data) is unconfirmed but likely fine for a simple directory. Net assessment: the asset transfers, the thin business does not.

## Recommended next step

**Pass.** The growth-lever kill gate is structural: the #1 way this business would acquire paying customers (SEO + personal X promotion) is exactly what this team cannot execute. Diligence won't change this gate — it would require a fundamental pivot in the acquisition model, which is a build problem, not a buy problem.

If the team wants a directory-style product in this space, building one on top of the team's existing organic reach (developer communities, product integrations, word-of-mouth) would be a better fit than buying one designed around channels they can't run.

At $500 the codebase has marginal scrap value as a boilerplate for a Next.js + Supabase + Stripe project — but that's not an acquisition thesis, it's a template purchase.
