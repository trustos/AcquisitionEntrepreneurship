## Fit-to-build read (the team's lens)

The team CAN maintain a Swift/SwiftUI + RevenueCat iOS app — that part is squarely in wheelhouse. The engineering upside (Android port, AI coaching overlay, better analytics, social accountability layer) is real and the team could execute it. **The problem is not operability — it's growth.** The only demonstrated acquisition channel is TikTok virality, which the team cannot reliably replicate or scale. ASO alone has generated ~$38/month of revenue. Paying $3,000 to acquire a $38/month business in a commodity niche with zero growth lever the team can operate is not a fit problem — it's a math problem. `growth_lever_fits` is UNKNOWN rather than FALSE only because ASO improvements are theoretically possible; in practice, the revenue data says ASO is not working.

## Does the value transfer? (key-person check)

The app product itself is impersonal by design (local data, no login) and should transfer cleanly. **The critical question is the TikTok channel.** The June spike almost certainly came from a viral TikTok video. If that was on the founder's personal account, the growth engine does not convey. The seller reports zero X/Twitter followers and no connected analytics — consistent with an abandoned or non-existent ongoing marketing operation. The 51 active subscribers are the only thing left after the viral moment, and their future behavior (renewal in ~12 months) is the entire remaining value.

## Recommended next step

**Pass on this deal.** The core thesis is broken: the only demonstrated growth lever (TikTok virality) is a one-time event that has already expired. The seller listed the app within 30 days of peak revenue while the decline was visible — a classic informed-seller adverse-selection. The $308 "MRR" is a RevenueCat accounting fiction obscuring ~$38/month of actual forward cash. At $3,000, you're paying 6.6× real forward ARR for a commodity app in a saturated niche with no moat. Even if you negotiated to $1,000 and all unknowns resolved positively, the growth problem remains: there is no scalable, team-operable acquisition channel shown to work for this product. The only scenario where this makes sense is if the TikTok account is a brand account with significant following that transfers — and even then, the team would need to hire/partner on TikTok content creation, which is outside their wheelhouse.

If somehow compelled to proceed: demand the RevenueCat export, TikTok account details, and a seller note covering at least 30% of the ask tied to subscriber retention over 12 months. Start at $800–$1,200.
